# Playbook — Romans in Stone shorts (35–55 s, vertical, narrated over paintings)

Source: `told-in-stone.md`. Caveat: Told in Stone posts no Shorts, so these rules
come from its shortest uploads and the cold opens of its top long videos.

1. **Put the puzzle in the first sentence, ≤ 3 s.** Name a concrete thing and a contradiction or a question ("this half of the Colosseum is gone. What happened?" — 6.6M). Never open with a channel name, "welcome" or "I'm…" (book trailer that did: 15k).
2. **Pick one of three hook shapes:** visible puzzle ("why is Rome buried?" 1.78M), superlative-then-reversal ("biggest, richest… also the most dangerous" 2.8M), or a myth you then debunk ("This never actually happened" 3.7M).
3. **Pin the hook to a date, a name or a number** (357 AD, 20 elephants, "10 ft or 3 m"). Concrete beats abstract.
4. **Script 90–120 words** (≈130–140 wpm with RyanNeural −5 %). Hard cap 125 words for 55 s.
5. **Structure: hook (0–3 s) → evidence image (3–12 s) → Roman voice/quote (12–35 s) → turn (35–45 s) → one-line takeaway (last 5–8 s).**
6. **One image per sentence, and the sentence must describe what's on screen** ("As this painting of the Colosseum illustrates…" 1.78M). If a line can't be illustrated with a painting in `content/art/`, cut or rewrite it.
7. **Quote the Roman briefly — ≤ 15 words of translation** (Latin optional, ≤ 4 words on screen), naming author and work in the line itself.
8. **Build in a turn** ("impressive though all this is…" 2.4M; "This never actually happened" 3.7M). A short with no reversal is a list.
9. **End on a compressed, slightly wry recap line** ("we have half a Colosseum because earthquakes and the pope spared it" 6.6M; "buried by its own rubble… and a little bit of dust in the wind" 1.78M). No Patreon/book/subscribe plug.
10. **Optional bookend:** close on the opening image or phrase (qMNx9-AjrXM, 2.3M), which also makes the short loop.
11. **Topics that pull views:** famous landmarks from an odd angle (Colosseum ×3 in top 10), squalid daily life (latrines, bad neighbourhoods, 2.4–2.8M), and loss/ruin/forgetting (1.7–6.6M). Time and ruin themes fit this.
12. **Title = short plain question, 4–9 words, no hype caps** (question titles median 277k vs 188k). E.g. "Why is Ancient Rome buried?"
13. **Description: first line gives the answer as a punchline** ("The short answer is: earthquakes and popes, in that order." 6.6M), then sources.
14. **Numbers in words the ear can grab** — one number per sentence, rounded, with a comparison ("two stories above the street").
15. **Engagement ask, if any, is a question to the viewer in ≤ 6 words** (1.78M used "If you have a question about the Greeks and Romans … let me know in the comments") — never more than that.
