{it|Tempus edax rerum.}

Every empire ends the same way. Eaten.

Rome's Forum was the heart of the world. By the Renaissance, cows grazed on it. Locals called it Campo Vaccino. The cow field.

Those three Latin words came from a Roman poet. Ovid, two thousand years ago.

Time, the consumer of all things.

It destroys everything, he wrote, "by the teeth of age, with a slow death."

Temples. Arches. A whole empire. Eaten one stone at a time.

But the emperor Marcus Aurelius found peace in it. "That time also is at hand," he wrote, "when thou thyself shalt be forgotten by all."

So do what is right, while you still are.

Time ate the Forum. And it is still hungry.
